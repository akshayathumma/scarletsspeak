What is Menstruation?

Menstruation (or a "period") is when your body releases blood and tissue from the uterus through the vagina. It usually happens once a month and can last anywhere between 3 to 7 days.

It’s your body’s way of saying, “Hey, no baby this month, so let’s reset!” You might feel different during this time — tired, emotional, or even totally normal. Everyone’s experience is valid. And no, it’s not dirty or weird. It’s your body doing exactly what it’s supposed to do.

Understanding the Menstrual Cycle

Your period is just one part of a larger cycle that your body goes through every month. It’s like your body running a routine — getting ready for pregnancy just in case, and resetting if it doesn’t happen.

The full cycle includes four phases:

  • Menstrual: When bleeding happens.
  • Follicular: The body starts preparing an egg.
  • Ovulation: The egg is released (usually around day 14).
  • Luteal: Hormones go up, and if there's no pregnancy, the cycle ends with another period.

Some people feel super energetic mid-cycle and more tired before their period. Tracking this helps you understand your emotions, energy levels, and body better.

Common Period Symptoms

Everyone experiences periods differently. For some, it’s just a bit of bleeding and that’s it. For others, it can feel like a storm of emotions and pain.

  • Cramps (like a tight knot in your belly)
  • Bloating (feeling puffier than usual)
  • Mood swings (suddenly emotional or irritated)
  • Acne or breakouts
  • Feeling tired or sleepy all day

You might also feel super hungry, crave sweets, or just want to lie down with a hot water bottle. It’s all normal. And if something feels too painful or not okay, talk to someone — don’t ignore your body.

Period Hygiene and Care

Staying clean and fresh during your period is important, but don’t overthink it — simple steps are enough:

  • Change your pad, tampon, or cup every 4–8 hours.
  • Wash your hands before and after changing products.
  • Use mild, unscented soap around your private parts.

If you use reusable products like cloth pads or menstrual cups, rinse and clean them properly. And remember — taking care of yourself is not just physical. Drink water, eat warm food, and wear comfy clothes!

Types of Period Products

There are so many options out there — what you use depends on what makes you feel safe, dry, and confident:

  • Pads: Stick to your underwear and absorb flow. Great for beginners.
  • Tampons: Inserted inside the vagina. Need practice, but super discreet.
  • Menstrual Cups: Reusable, eco-friendly, and can last up to 8–12 hours.
  • Period Panties: Look like normal underwear but with built-in layers to absorb flow.

There’s no "right" product. You might have to try a few before finding your favorite — and that’s okay.

Tracking Your Cycle

Knowing when your period starts, how long it lasts, and how you feel each day helps you understand your body better.

You can:

  • Use period tracking apps (like Clue, Flo, etc.)
  • Mark dates in a journal or calendar
  • Write down how you feel (mood, pain, flow, cravings)

Over time, you’ll see patterns: maybe you get moody before your period or super creative mid-cycle. Tracking helps you plan better and spot when something feels off.

Period Myths vs. Facts

  • Myth: You can’t touch plants or cook during your period.
    Fact: Yes, you can. Periods don’t affect your magical powers.
  • Myth: You’re not clean during your period.
    Fact: Your body is just doing its job. There’s nothing unclean about it.
  • Myth: Boys shouldn’t know about periods.
    Fact: Everyone should. It’s part of life and affects half the world’s population.

Taking Care of Your Mental Health

Periods don’t just affect your body — they can mess with your feelings too. Some people feel down, angry, anxious, or just “off” a few days before and during their period.

Tips that might help:

  • Journal your feelings — let it out.
  • Rest when you’re low on energy.
  • Talk to someone who understands.
  • Do something comforting — music, hot shower, warm food.

Your emotions are valid. You’re not “too sensitive” or “lazy” — you’re just human.

When Should You See a Doctor?

  • Bleeding that soaks through a pad/tampon every 1–2 hours
  • Cramps so bad you can’t get out of bed
  • No periods for 3+ months (and you’re not pregnant)
  • Periods that last more than 7 days
  • Sudden changes in flow or pain

Don’t wait till things get worse. You deserve care and answers. Listening to your body is the best kind of self-love.

Real Talk: Things No One Tells You

  • You might cry over a puppy video — and that’s okay.
  • You might hate your body one day and love it the next. That’s okay too.
  • Period leaks happen — it’s annoying but fixable.
  • Some people feel more creative, while others feel foggy.
  • It’s okay to rest, cancel plans, or say “I need a break today.”

Periods don’t make you weak — they make you powerful, aware, and in tune with your body.
